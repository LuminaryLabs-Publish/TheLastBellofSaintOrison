# Town Gate — Location Design

**Target specification; existing playable content is unchanged.**

## Purpose and geography

Read the crossing notice, then open a safe route into town.

Arrival from a new journey on the sea road; departure to visitor-centre. Reuse the current authored environment and entry/exit identities. New controls occupy reachable close-inspection views rather than requiring new locomotion.

## First readable situation

A trolley waits behind a stop bar; the gate lamp has a moving mechanical shutter.

## Authored interaction zones

- Zone 1: **Read the Crossing Signal**. Find when the crossing is safe. Controls: shelter, cycle-drum, crossing-marker. A wide view must expose its location before a close view is required.
- Zone 2: **Release the Gate Latch**. Open the gate without supplying a living name. Controls: badge-slot, tension-handle, catch. A wide view must expose its location before a close view is required.
- Zone 3: **Answer the Callbox**. Check whether the apparent voice is a live person. Controls: microphone-cover, spool-window, return-arrows. A wide view must expose its location before a close view is required.

## Spatial constraints

Keep the exit and named shelter visible from the primary interaction zone. No required control may sit behind a permanently locked optional object. Reused return environments keep their landmark positions; state variants change lighting and access, not the meaning of established navigation symbols. Initial blockout uses labelled zones; final dimensions and camera framing need a measured asset/layout review.

## Existing atmosphere to retain

Salt gathers on the envelope in your coat. Elian’s cassette arrived yesterday, though she has been missing for eleven days. Ahead, the gatehouse light moves across an empty crossing. Beyond it stands the hospital’s bell tower. You are Mara Venn. You came to find your sister, not to give this place another name.

This paragraph is archival narrative context, not a required arrival popup in the target design. Deliver its essential facts through the encounter actions; retain full writing in the optional journal.

## Existing safe retreat

You step behind the stone recess. Something crosses where you were standing. It never enters the marked shelter.
