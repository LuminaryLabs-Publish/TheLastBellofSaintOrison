# Narrative treatment: The Last Bell of Saint Orison

Mara Venn arrives with a cassette sent by Elian, her sister, eleven days after Elian disappeared. A private phrase and a recorded sequence of a gull cry and two bells make it credible. Mara wants to find a person, not save a town or become a hero.

Saint Orison's public story is evacuation following infrastructure failure. The historical record underneath it describes twelve hospital children and a deliberate oxygen shutoff at 02:13. The bell holds patterns created from suppressed identities. Adults have confused the preservation of an echo with care for an actual person.

Elian entered voluntarily to interrupt Orison Recovery's plan to relay the pattern through municipal infrastructure. A physical annex beside the Chapel holds her; the archive line is a communication route, not a way to teleport. Mara can help release her there or provide a verified return route later. Unconfirmed safety remains unconfirmed in the ending.

The thirteenth identity is the vessel for erased memory, **not a thirteenth child**. Four early identities are June Arlen, Tomas Reed, Ada Wren and Niko Bell. The later four in this adaptation are Iris Cole, Samir Hale, Ruth Fen and Leon March. Chosen aliases are preserved beside institutional names; knowing a name does not give Mara ownership of a person's memory.

The public relay can be kept intact or deliberately broken at the Tower. Original evidence can be recovered at the Archive. The Well's private instructions explain three interventions before commitment:

- Witness uses original proof, eight verified names and an intact relay to distribute an inspectable record. Memory no longer has to be kept in one vessel.
- Silence isolates the mechanism with the documented outer/inner/outer method. The voices stop; the player acknowledges that surviving proof is incomplete.
- Vessel copies Mara's own pattern into the apparatus. The town quiets but the container still exists; other people can lose the ability to retain her name.

The return demonstrates the cost: changed access, uncertainty about Elian, and a public record that distinguishes observation from inference. The final choice of words is part of the game. Mara leaves unknowns visible rather than replacing concealment with a new confident fiction.

## Adaptation decisions

The recovered source was an existing five-part manuscript. This build expands it into the approved fifteen-location linear journey, with local alternatives rather than five completely separate campaigns. The original private source document is not republished here.

The institutional-memory explanation, Mara/Elian relationship, private phrase, Orison Recovery, eight-name proof condition and three endings are retained. The town locations and last four names are authored adaptation choices. The original optional coercive transfer into an executive is omitted; Vessel is a voluntary self-transfer here. The cast is conveyed through narration and quoted dialogue in the current build; physical character animation and performed voices remain production work.

The approved 180-minute budgets are design targets. Current writing and interactions do not establish that duration. A blind playtest must measure it; additional conversations, tactile puzzle operations and staged discoveries should add meaningful content rather than artificial waiting.
